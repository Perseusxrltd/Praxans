# A 300-person founding model — 8 October 2026

**Three hundred is a chosen starting population, not evidence of carrying capacity or a survival guarantee.** The implementation collaborator selected it after the user requested settlements of a few hundred people. At the time of this review, the earlier 32-adult restoration remained unapplied. This note records the consequences of using 300 with the inspected rules; later implementation must identify which assumptions changed.

The current founder routine creates adults and pairs adjacent entries. Renewal assigns ages 18–42. Consequently, **300 under that routine means 300 adult arrivals**, not a measured cross-section of 300 people including children and elders. An all-adult migration can be an explicit intervention assumption. It should not be described as a typical ancient village, a self-sufficient breeding population, or a natural demographic equilibrium. Do not invent an age pyramid or effective genetic population from headcount alone.

The observations below preserve the initial implementation review. The later [connected-model review](connected-model-review.md) records which placement, access, geometry and cache defects were corrected and gives the completed short-run comparison. Neither revision establishes long-term demographic adequacy.

## Historical questions and empirical checks

Xenophon's household provisions and the _Arthashastra_'s store accounts (`A04`, `A08`) suggest separating stock, replenishment, processing and loss. Hippocratic attention to water, seasons and place (`A03`) motivates local source inspection. Ibn Khaldun's example of several crafts contributing to grain preparation (`A09`) asks which tasks and carriers make a division of labor possible. None specifies a universally viable founding population or authorizes a fixed social hierarchy.

The modern checks already consulted remain deliberately narrow: FAO grain-storage conditions (`M03`), IPCC standing biomass versus biomass growth (`M01`), and measured Hadza travel/energy in a particular sample (`M02`). Hill et al.'s reported mean of 28.2 adults in residential bands (`M04`, abstract only) concerns residence within social networks, not a minimum viable isolated population and not evidence against all larger settlements. A forest's dry-matter stock is not its annual edible production.

No externally calibrated daily water requirement is asserted here. WHO, FAO energy, EFSA water and Sphere requests attempted during this batch returned 403; a guessed WEDC technical-note path returned 404. Those inaccessible sources are not counted as consulted evidence. The water numbers below come from the code and need empirical calibration before being called adequate human provision.

Demand rises by **9.375 times**. At the present material densities, all the 300-person opening stocks occupy approximately **718.04 m³**, including **654.55 m³ of generic food biomass** at 220 kg/m³. That density describes the model's broad material category; it is not measured packed-grain density. After wrapping or packing rations, count the changed locations of those same materials.

`decayStocks` compares actual constructed storage volume with the volume of all camp stocks. Three hundred people therefore need much more constructed protection even when food per person is unchanged. Large supplies do not create a storehouse, containers, support or processing capacity. Grain moisture and material form remain unresolved distinctions in the generic stock model.

At its maximum, the present seasonal heuristic requests **518.581 kg/person**, or **155,574.36 kg for 300 adults**, from `1.6 × expm1(0.006 × 180) / 0.006`. Its parameters are effective assumptions. The 144 t opening food can conceal a production deficit for a long time; a run must report what fraction of consumption comes from the original arrival rather than only whether anyone is still alive.

## Catchment, production and travel must scale together

A complete integer-centred 120 m disk contains 441 current cells, or **4.41 ha of cell footprints**. The ideal geometric disk has 4.524 ha. Three hundred adults divided by the ideal disk gives approximately **6,631 adults/km² of catchment**. This is a ratio within the selected domain, not a settlement-density estimate or a calibrated carrying capacity.

Keeping the old area per founder would require a radius of about **367.4 m**, covering 42.41 ha in an ideal disk. This preserves a ratio only; it does not establish adequate food production, fresh water, usable land or access. Increasing radius without changing travel, knowledge and storage can add inaccessible resources to a diagnostic count.

The original `gatherTask` stopped at 120 m and exploration at 100 m. At inspection, renewal validation also capped habitat radius at 24 cells/240 m and people at 128. The collaborator is now replacing the fixed search with each person's actual memories, a travel budget and bounded local exploration. That is implementation work in progress, not yet a successful 300-person result in this note.

For perspective, the baseline hunger range alone would require **16.15–21.74 tonnes of delivered food per hectare per year** if all 300 were supported by the 4.41 ha footprint. That excludes thermal costs and storage losses. It must not be justified by comparing it with standing timber or counting the opening food as annual production. The IPCC 4 t dry above-ground biomass/ha/year default for older temperate continental forests concerns a different quantity; it is useful precisely because those quantities must not be confused.

A 48 kg main-layer plus 6 kg understory initialization across 441 empty eligible cells would add 23,814 kg of organic tissue, before minerals and seeds. Actual additions depend on water/shore exclusions, pre-existing plants and template eligibility. This total is neither all edible nor a yearly flow. Establishment at a broader radius remains an explicit finite intervention, not automatic regrowth or proof of a mature ecosystem.

Use complete saved catchments and distinguish represented, traversable, observed, reachable and harvested areas. If new terrain must be materialized, record it as the existing boundary mechanism requires. A camera opening a wider map must not perform that operation. Cross-region pathfinding, remembered water, carried food and return journeys must agree on the same coordinates and units.

## Labor and local water constraints

At initial skill 1, a successful food gathering task can take at most **6.36 kg**, subject to tissue/mineral availability and extraction policy. Its 2.4 progress-hours imply **73.6–99.1 nominal progress-hours/day** to meet the 300-person baseline hunger range if every trip reaches that cap. These are optimistic throughput bounds. Actual progress is scaled by diligence, effort and attention; travel, depleted patches, processing, care, sleep, tools and other work add costs. More workers cannot harvest tissue that did not grow.

At 1.8 km/hour before terrain and fatigue, a 367.4 m outward-and-return trip costs about 0.408 hours of walking. This illustrates why travel must be charged, not why that radius is sufficient. Track delivered food per person-hour and per patch, along with failed trips and the distribution of access, rather than granting automatic productivity from population.

Baseline drinking losses of 468–561.6 kg/day need **accessible replenishment**, not merely a large water scalar somewhere in the domain. A 2,000 kg site threshold represents only about 3.6–4.3 days of that flow without replenishment or other uses. A 16,000 kg wet-cell water capacity is a model pool, not a potable spring discharge or an aquifer yield. Measure liquid versus frozen water, source depletion/recharge, travel and waiting, melt-energy consumption, and failed access. The current drinking operation has no explicit container, queue, household water delivery or sanitary demand; those absences must not be advertised as realistic provision.

Camp food access is within 2.4 cells/24 m; personal provisions hold up to 3 kg. Scattering newcomers should therefore preserve actual access to food and water, or transfer finite provisions to them explicitly. Nine hundred kilograms packed across 300 people leaves camp stock but remains food within the community. It is not consumed merely because the camp stock decreased.

## Later update: spatial concentration and heat

After the initial review above, the parent reported a **300-adult-per-camp full-backup trial**: all 1,200 arrivals remained alive at day 14, but camp centers overheated and camp food stocks fell by roughly 29–30 t. Later inspection of the completed artifact establishes initial temperatures around 20.2°C and final temperatures of 44.12–49.19°C. The parent's early description called the stock difference “spoilage”; the recorded quantity is **net stock drawdown**, which must be separated from consumption, harvest and transfers before attributing it to decay. This is short-run evidence of a serious resource/thermal problem, not confirmation that founding is adequate.

The proposed explanation is excessive concentration: rest assignments returned residents to one point, and the previous `decayStocks` sent every spoiled item to that same 100 m² cell. Respiration and decomposition release chemical energy into the occupied tile. The parent subsequently implemented distributed rest and exposed-stock placement over a footprint based on population and material volume. The [completed comparison](connected-model-review.md#completed-300-person-short-comparison) records 21.88–22.93°C camp centers, 130.00–134.02 t remaining food per camp and no new deaths at day 14. Several mechanisms changed together, so this supports the changed bundle rather than isolating one cause or proving seasonal balance.

The inspected food baseline for 300 adults, 195–262.5 kg/day, corresponds to approximately **36.1–48.6 kW** of released chemical heat through `food × 0.94 × 17,000 kJ/kg`. If all that heat is deposited in one 100 m² cell, it is about **361–486 W/m²**, before decomposition and environmental transfers. Spread over 4,800 m², the same input is about 7.5–10.1 W/m². These are derived heat-input bounds, not predicted temperatures: actual timing, respiration locations, evaporation, radiation and transport matter. Thirty tonnes of food contains roughly 479.4 GJ of represented organic chemical energy. This calculation does not establish that 30 t spoiled or decomposed in the trial; spoilage would not release all its energy instantly in any case.

The draft footprint uses **16 m² per person** and **0.5 m open-pile height** as explicit model assumptions. For 300 people, the population term gives 4,800 m², or a 39.1 m ideal radius; the initial stock-volume term gives about 1,436 m². Neither constant has been empirically calibrated here, and neither grants shelter, storage protection or carrying capacity.

The immediate comparison should record per-cell resting occupancy, allocated stocks and deposited waste, food consumption/spoilage, respiratory/decomposition heat, latent transfers, and camp-integrated thermal change. Keep total arrival material and the initial source/request fingerprints identical. Lower maximum temperature alone is insufficient if the implementation has simply removed energy or relocated material without a physical account.

Several connected constraints need explicit checks:

- A footprint must account for actual usable land. **Correction:** the earlier square-neighborhood warning was wrong; `nearbyTiles` filters a disk. The later allocation targets `ceil(area/100)` connected cells within the radius-plus-one-cell search, and the probe confirms 48 cells for 300 people on open land. A shoreline or obstacle can still constrain achieved area. Ownership and overlapping uses remain separate questions.
- Distributed waste implies distributed stocks or actual hauling. Equal allocation over a changing footprint is an effective subgrid storage assumption; it is not a record of individual piles moving themselves as the population changes. Track such redistribution and avoid duplicating stock in both aggregate and local pools.
- The initial 39.1 m radius extended beyond the old **24 m** food/refill/wrapping access. The inspected correction now uses the shared footprint for these paths and for child/idle rest. A later cardinal-adjacency correction also rejects stock access from disconnected diagonal land. These fixes preserve the requirement for reachable food rather than granting access by the footprint's name.
- A large pile presently adds decomposable fuel without increasing `heatCapacity`, which includes an effective base plus water/ice/snow but not food, detritus or structure mass. Terrestrial decomposition is oxygen-limited against the **global** atmospheric pool, with no resolved diffusion through a compact pile. These are further scale/transport limits, not calibrated compost physics.
- Food-loss temperature sensitivity and hourly decomposition can amplify spatial concentration. Compare heat and material traces before changing their physical rates. Do not clamp temperature to a desired survival range.

Candidate `R24` scopes that diagnosis. The [construction audit](open-construction.md) separately shows why storage protection needs actual enclosure; its connected-volume correction now passes the scoped checks in the [follow-up review](connected-model-review.md). Actual contents, loading/retrieval, material heat capacity, processing/tools and knowledge carriers remain open. The completed combined **32-founder** 600-day run ended with only four people in Amber and three in Stone, so the optimistic separate runs and improved 300-person short comparison must not be promoted into a general survival claim.
