# Agency, success, and contact

Accepted direction, 2026-10-07: **external agents gain influence through trust and institutions; Praxans may refuse their proposals.** This contract expands the [implementation ledger](roadmap.md). It describes the target depth intervention and explicitly separates its first primitives from later societal systems.

## Authority belongs to inhabitants

An agent is a persistent adviser. It can propose priorities, spending, experiments, construction, repairs, correspondence, exchanges, commitments and expeditions. Proposals do not immediately change policy, spend another community's resources, move a person, or alter natural laws. People consider feasibility, their needs, existing obligations, trust and disagreement. A refused proposal is a valid outcome.

All keys for a community share one simulation-time deliberation budget. Creating extra connections does not buy extra authority. The receipt acknowledges submission; later observation gives the accepted, refused, expired or failed result. Network retries must not create a second proposal.

The first institution is a small local assembly, an explicit starting social convention for these early communities. Participation requires a living adult near the meeting place. Quorum and consent are inspectable, and accepted institutional proposals can change bounded decision rules. They cannot bypass bodily needs, physical feasibility, individual participation or the proposal budget. Deeper elected offices, delegation, enforcement, succession, factions and secession remain separate work.

Trust is a bounded memory of experience, not a spendable currency. People evaluate what follows accepted advice over time. This is observational feedback under changing weather and circumstances, not a claim that every outcome was caused by an agent. High trust may make a proposal persuasive; it never guarantees acceptance. Agents cannot override a refusal by putting imperative language into a reason or letter.

## Political and social forms emerge

Explicit user direction, 2026-10-08: Praxans must be able to organize politically and socially freely. Trust, kinship, shared work, possession and access to resources, persuasion, dependence and conflict supply the conditions. People may form overlapping groups, follow or contest leaders, delegate limited authority, negotiate rules, leave associations, split communities and maintain competing institutions. Authority may be earned, inherited, delegated, contested or seized through represented actions and consequences. No government name or progression unlock establishes powers.

The next primitive is a relationship among actual participants: membership and scoped authorization, with holders, a purpose, duration, conditions, communication and revocation. A group needs people who recognize or enforce its commitments; a role cannot conjure obedience, supplies or remote knowledge. Distance, dissent, a holder's absence or death, disputed succession and withdrawal must change what can actually happen. Coercion requires represented control and its costs, rather than an automatic exception to agency.

The present single local assembly per civilization is a provisional starting convention. It is not a natural law, a universal political form or a claim that population growth should preserve one meeting forever. Replacing it requires explicit migration of existing commitments and earned relationships, bounded local participation and persistent group histories. External agents keep their advisory role while their influence develops through the organizations inhabitants actually create. H06 in the ledger tracks the unresolved work; this section is an accepted requirement, not a claim that these organizations already exist.

The [membership and mandate review](research/open-social-organization-review.md) separates affiliation, custody, consent and authority. The [autonomous association proposal](research/autonomous-associations-review.md) starts with residents requesting help on witnessed, funded work, actual encounters, individual acceptance and attributable contributions. Renewed arrangements may support overlapping groups and limited coordination; isolation, refusal or useful independent work may produce none. Neither a compulsory government template nor an external agent is required for that proposed process.

## Success has several dimensions

The shared objective is to build a civilization that succeeds and continues to develop. What success means remains contestable. The engine reports a vector rather than a compulsory scalar winner:

- **Wellbeing:** living people's health, nourishment, rest and satisfaction, including the least well supplied.
- **Resilience:** food reserves, shelter and ability to withstand interruptions; short-term accumulation has diminishing returns.
- **Knowledge:** evidence-backed ideas held by living people, their retention and transmission; repeating an experiment or renaming a design does not automatically earn more reward.
- **Ecology:** local living cover and functional diversity relative to the relevant measurement baseline, with depletion visible.
- **Connection:** actual reciprocal contact, reliable exchanges and kept commitments; sending messages by itself is not success.
- **Reach:** a civilization's represented population and sustained material/territorial presence, with its costs visible in the other dimensions.

A community may adopt its own aspiration and weighting of these dimensions. This affects its interpretation of progress, not the physical results or a server-wide leaderboard. No chosen ambition erases deaths, damaged ecosystems or broken promises from the record.

Feedback is sampled in simulation time. Signed changes between measurements expose both gains and losses; polling cannot mint points. Once-only high-water achievements provide durable milestones without a repeated reward for destroying and rebuilding the same thing. Milestones never grant food, stronger materials, faster labor or authority. Current condition remains visible beside historical records. Age and the date of the first measurement accompany results so new and ancient communities are not silently compared as if they had equal histories.

## Contact is evidence, not a universal menu

Each community has its own record of other groups: first/last contact, direct encounters, dated reports, comprehension, affinity and confidence. A newly founded civilization does not receive every camp's private inventories. Seeing travelers, meeting them, sharing repeated conversations, exchanging goods and keeping commitments provide different evidence. Familiarity can fade; positive affinity and language comprehension are distinct.

Contact labels summarize these facts. They are not a compulsory civilization progression or a magic unlock tree. A tentative encounter can lead to a gift, misunderstanding or hostility; a familiar partner can become an enemy. A distant public spectator view does not confer a physical communication route.

Reports describe what a messenger or visitor learned at a particular time. They must not silently update when the other group's population or stores change. People can act on outdated information. Strong competitive secrecy is not promised while the spectator world is public, but the simulation still requires a carrier for interactions.

## Open conversation and concrete commitments

Agents can write their own diplomatic text, arguments, offers and replies. Text is inert data: it is never executed as an instruction by the simulation. A sentence such as “we own your land now” does not transfer land.

Negotiable commitments use composable primitives with parties, quantities, duration and deadlines. The first useful set is peace, passage and deliveries of represented goods. Cooperation, aid, tribute, exchange and alliances can be expressed through conversation and combinations of these commitments. Full military coordination, joint construction, multilateral institutions and arbitrary legal adjudication need further mechanics; prose alone does not implement them.

A commitment requires the participating communities' decisions and communication. The sender's interface cannot accept on behalf of the recipient. A promise is not magical enforcement: goods still require stock, people, capacity and travel. Failure, expiry, refusal and observed breach change the relevant relationship and remain in history. Third parties do not instantly inherit omniscient reputation.

## Distance, exchange, and conflict have costs

Messages and exchanges need a traversable route and elapsed time. Material traffic must have real cargo and finite capacity. A requested export cannot be removed from another camp before its inhabitants consider the request. Outbound delivery and return goods are separate parts of a journey. Scarcity or a broken route can invalidate a previously promising offer.

Conflict uses the same world. An agent can argue for hostility; inhabitants can refuse it. A physical expedition needs willing living adults, provisions, a route, time away from other work, and acceptance of injury or death. Resources change hands only through represented contact and transport. Peace and cooperation remain possible before, during and after hostility. There is no remote attack button or reward for writing a war declaration.

The initial expedition model is a limited raid/encounter primitive, not a complete military simulation. Weapons, fortifications, maneuvers, logistics networks, occupation, political coercion and post-conflict institutions are separate ledger entries, governed by the same constraints when implemented.

## Verification contract

Tests must demonstrate refusal, delayed implementation, shared per-community limits, stale reports, unknown-target rejection, travel delay, reciprocal consent, resource closure, failed exchanges, observed breach and outcome feedback without polling or repetition rewards. Persistence tests must preserve proposals, messages, commitments, learning and achievements through restart. Migration introduces these measurements at its actual tick and does not invent earlier trust, meetings, discoveries or achievements.
